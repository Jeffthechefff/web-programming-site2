History of the Internet Timeline

The Internet is a worldwide network of interconnected networks that communicate using shared protocols such as TCP/IP. It carries services including email, file transfers and the World Wide Web. This timeline follows its development from early research to broadband, wireless access and today’s global connectivity.

1958 — Creation of ARPA

1958 — Creation of ARPA

In 1957, the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1. In response, in 1958, the United States created the Advanced Research Projects Agency (ARPA) within the Department of Defense. ARPA later supported research that contributed to the development of computer networking.

Reference: https://www.darpa.mil/about/innovation-timeline

1962 — The Idea of a Global Computer Network

1962 — The Idea of a Global Computer Network

J.C.R. Licklider, a researcher associated with ARPA, described ideas about a globally interconnected set of computers. His vision involved computers communicating with one another and allowing people to access information and resources from different locations.

These ideas influenced later research into computer networking.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1965 — First Wide-Area Computer Network Experiment

1965 — First Wide-Area Computer Network Experiment

In 1965, researchers Lawrence Roberts and Thomas Merrill connected a computer in Massachusetts with another computer located in California. The experiment demonstrated that computers in distant locations could communicate through a telephone line.

The experiment also provided researchers with practical experience in communicating between geographically separated computers.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1969 — ARPANET Goes Online

1969 — ARPANET Goes Online

In 1969, ARPANET connected its first four host computers. The initial locations included UCLA, the Stanford Research Institute, UC Santa Barbara, and the University of Utah.

This network became one of the most important foundations for later Internet development.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1971 — Electronic Mail

1971 — Electronic Mail

In 1971, Ray Tomlinson developed a program that allowed electronic messages to be sent between computers connected through ARPANET.

He also selected the "@" symbol as part of the format used for identifying the destination of an email message.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1973 — International Connections

1973 — International Connections

ARPANET began making international connections in the 1970s. In 1973, networks in the United Kingdom and Norway were connected to ARPANET.

These connections demonstrated the importance of developing networking technologies that could work across different countries and independent networks.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1974 — TCP is Proposed

1974 — TCP is Proposed

In 1974, Vint Cerf and Bob Kahn published a paper describing a method for connecting different packet-switched networks. Their work contributed to the development of the Transmission Control Protocol, which later became part of TCP/IP.

The basic idea was to allow separate networks to communicate without requiring every network to use the same internal technology.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1983 — TCP/IP Becomes the Standard

1983 — TCP/IP Becomes the Standard

On January 1, 1983, ARPANET officially switched from its previous networking protocol to TCP/IP.

This transition was significant because TCP/IP provided a standard method for different networks to communicate. It became a fundamental part of the architecture of the Internet.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1986 — NSFNET Is Established

1986 — NSFNET Is Established

In 1986, the United States National Science Foundation established NSFNET. It was designed to connect researchers and supercomputer centers and became an important part of the expanding academic network infrastructure.

NSFNET later played an important role in the expansion of Internet connectivity in the United States.

Reference: https://www.nsf.gov/impacts/internet

1995 — Commercial Networks Replace the NSFNET Backbone

1995 — Commercial Networks Replace the NSFNET Backbone

The NSFNET backbone was retired in 1995 as commercial networks took over its role. This transition supported the growth of a commercially operated Internet.

Reference: https://www.nsf.gov/impacts/internet

1997 — The Foundation of Wi-Fi

1997 — The Foundation of Wi-Fi

IEEE published the original 802.11 wireless local-area networking standard in 1997. It laid the technical foundation for Wi-Fi, allowing devices to connect to local networks without a network cable. A router can connect that local network to the Internet.

Reference: https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/802.11/1163/

2000s — Broadband Becomes More Common

2000s — Broadband Becomes More Common

Home broadband adoption rose during the 2000s; Pew’s US surveys document this shift away from dial-up. Faster home connections made downloading, multimedia and online communication more practical. Adoption happened at different speeds across countries.

Reference: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/fact-sheet/internet-broadband/

2007 — Smartphones Expand Mobile Internet Use

2007 — Smartphones Expand Mobile Internet Use

Apple introduced the iPhone in 2007 with Internet features including email and Web browsing. It became an important example of the smartphone shift toward portable Internet access. Mobile Internet existed before the iPhone.

Reference: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/

2009 — Commercial 4G/LTE Services

2009 — Commercial 4G/LTE Services

In December 2009, TeliaSonera launched commercial LTE services marketed as 4G in Stockholm and Oslo. Faster mobile broadband helped extend Internet use beyond fixed connections.

Reference: https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2009/12/14/147245/0/en/TeliaSonera-first-in-the-world-with-4G-services.html

2012 — World IPv6 Launch

2012 — World IPv6 Launch

On 6 June 2012, participating network operators, websites and equipment manufacturers permanently enabled IPv6. Its much larger address space supports continued Internet growth as more devices connect.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/deploy360/ipv6/

2020s — 5G Networks Expand

2020s — 5G Networks Expand

By September 2020, 3GPP reported that 89 operators had launched commercial 5G services. This new generation of mobile networking expanded the possibilities for mobile broadband and connected devices. Deployment and availability differ across regions.

Reference: https://www.3gpp.org/ftp/Information/Highlights/2020_Issue01/2020_09_3gpp_highlights01_optimized.pdf

2026 — Internet Access Today

2026 — Internet Access Today

The Internet now supports everyday communication through fixed and mobile networks. ITU’s 2025 report estimates that almost three-quarters of the world’s population is online, while 2.2 billion people remain offline. As of this October 2026 update, affordable and reliable access remains an important challenge.

Reference: https://www.itu.int/itu-d/reports/statistics/facts-figures-2025/
