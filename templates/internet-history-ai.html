History of the Internet (AI) Timeline

This seven-milestone timeline complements the regular Internet history page. It covers internetworking research, domain names, commercialization, broadband, mobile access, and cloud computing.

Hover over a box to read its details, or select “Keep details visible”. Keyboard users can press Space on the checkbox.

1973 - TCP/IP Development Begins

1973 - TCP/IP Development Begins

During the early 1970s, researchers working with DARPA began investigating how separate computer networks could communicate with one another. Vint Cerf and Robert Kahn developed the fundamental ideas behind TCP/IP, a system of protocols designed to allow different networks to exchange information. This concept was essential to creating an interconnected "network of networks," which is the basic idea behind the Internet.

www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet-related-networks/

1984 - Domain Name System (DNS) Is Introduced

1984 - Domain Name System (DNS) Is Introduced

As the number of computers connected to networks increased, it became impractical for people to remember numerical IP addresses for every computer. The Domain Name System (DNS) provided a hierarchical naming system that allowed computers and services to be identified using easier-to-remember names. DNS became an important part of making the growing Internet easier for people to use.

www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/brief-history-internet/

1995 — NSFNET's Backbone Role Ends

1995 — NSFNET's Backbone Role Ends

In 1995, NSFNET stopped operating as the primary backbone for the Internet. Commercial Internet networks had expanded and were increasingly carrying Internet traffic.

This period marked an important transition toward a more commercially operated Internet.

Reference: https://www.nsf.gov/impacts/internet

2000s — The Growth of Broadband

2000s — The Growth of Broadband

During the 2000s, broadband Internet connections became increasingly common. Faster connections made it easier for people to access websites, multimedia content, online communication, and other Internet services.

The growth of broadband also contributed to the expansion of online services and digital media.

Reference: https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/

2007 — The Smartphone Era Expands

2007 — The Smartphone Era Expands

The introduction of modern smartphones helped make Internet access increasingly mobile. Mobile devices allowed users to access websites and online services without being connected to a traditional desktop computer.

The growth of smartphones contributed to the transformation of the Internet into a service that could be accessed from many different locations.

Reference: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2007/01/09Apple-Reinvents-the-Phone-with-iPhone/

2010s - Cloud Computing and Mobile Internet Expand

2010s - Cloud Computing and Mobile Internet Expand

During the 2010s, cloud computing became increasingly important, allowing users and organizations to access computing resources, storage, and software through Internet-connected services. At the same time, smartphones and mobile networks became major ways of accessing online services. The Internet consequently became deeply integrated into communication, education, entertainment, business, and everyday services.

www.nist.gov/publications/nist-definition-cloud-computing

Today — A Global Network

Today — A Global Network

Today, the Internet connects billions of people and devices around the world. It supports communication, education, research, commerce, entertainment, cloud computing, and many other activities.

The modern Internet continues to evolve as new networking technologies, devices, applications, and services are developed.

Reference: https://www.internetsociety.org/internet/history-internet/
